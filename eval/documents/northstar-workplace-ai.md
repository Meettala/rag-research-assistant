Northstar Analytics: Workplace AI Adoption Report

Northstar Analytics is a synthetic organisation created for evaluation purposes only. Nothing in this document describes a real company, real people or real financial results. It exists so that retrieval and abstention behaviour can be measured against known ground truth.

The report examines how Northstar Analytics introduced assistive language models across three internal departments during a twelve month pilot. The pilot began in the customer operations team, expanded to the internal documentation team, and finished with a limited trial in the finance reporting team.

Customer operations reported the clearest improvement. Average first response time on written support tickets fell from 14 hours to 6 hours. Agents used the assistant to draft replies, which a human then reviewed and edited before sending. No reply was ever sent without a human reading it first. Ticket volume itself did not change during the pilot.

The internal documentation team used the assistant to summarise long engineering notes into shorter reference pages. Reviewers found that roughly one summary in eight contained a claim that the source notes did not support. Because of this, the team adopted a rule that every generated summary must link back to the specific source note it was drawn from, and that unsupported sentences must be deleted rather than rewritten.

The finance reporting team ran the most restricted trial. The assistant was given read access to a synthetic ledger extract and was asked to describe trends in plain language. It was never permitted to calculate a figure that would then be published. Every number that appeared in a finance deliverable was computed by the existing reporting pipeline, not by the model. The team described this boundary as non negotiable.

Across all three departments, the pilot identified a recurring failure pattern. When a question fell outside the supplied material, the assistant produced a fluent and confident answer anyway. Reviewers described this as the single most damaging behaviour observed, because a wrong answer delivered confidently is harder to catch than an obvious error.

Northstar Analytics concluded the pilot with four recommendations. First, retrieval quality should be measured separately from answer quality. Second, systems should be able to decline. Third, every published claim needs a traceable source. Fourth, staff training should focus on reviewing model output rather than on writing prompts.

The pilot did not evaluate voice interfaces, image generation, code generation, or any use of customer personal data. Those areas were explicitly excluded from scope before the pilot began.
